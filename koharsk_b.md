Kahara was worried about Proto. The novice, he corrected himself. Kera.

They had just boarded the assault carrier. Next to his shoulder, the ramp slammed shut, sealing them in the red-lit crew bay. In front of him, Kera was fumbling with the restraints, trying to lock them in place. Kahara made a move to stow his rifle and help, when the fastener clicked. She checked it again, gauntlets pulling at the clamps. The engine roared, and the vehicle began turning on the spot. The tracks ground against the concrete a span underneath him. Aest would be driving them out shortly, visor on periscope and supplemental camera feeds on his peripherals. The next time the ramp opened, they would be walking into the guns of a prepared enemy.

She wasn’t ready. It had taken her long to learn the suit. Oh, she was competent now, but barely-qualified competence might crack under pressure. And breaching was so much more than just moving in actuated armor. It was forming a tactical whole with your squad, a machine with each part synchronized to the others. It was handling the information overload from the sensors and the tactical data, while others were doing their thrice-slagged best to kill you. _And_ it was moving in actuated armor while doing all this.

In front of him, Kera turned her helmed head towards the closed ramp. Front and above him, he corrected himself. She was a little on the tall side for a girl, while Kahara was well below the one-point-eight span average. He had never minded. He got well along with anybody worth getting along with, and out-jested the rest. And out-ran, the few times when that failed.

Kera’s visor spun towards the bay. He nodded at her in sympathy, smiling  even though she wouldn’t be able to see it through his visor. It was only three weeks since they’d drunk the Furnace with her. He remembered standing on the grounds the next morning, reaching out to hold her hair while she stood doubled over, hands on knees, retching bile and the remains of last night’s booze into the grass. She’d waved his hand away. Dalen had pretended not to notice. Officially, they were all fit for work.

Only three weeks, he thought, his smile at the memory fading. He had had at least two months himself. Maybe three, and he’d still been scared shitless on his first breach. But something needed breaching today, and the third were the next in feed to do it.

If all went well, she’d be a breacher after this morning. That was what the Furnace folks had estimated likely to happen, and what the squad had prepared for, hidden from Kera. Failing that, she’d be reassigned somewhere else for retraining or re-evaluation, or for her body or her mind to be patched back together. The last scenario was rare, but he’d heard it happening. Everybody reacted differently to the rockslide of sensory information that was breaching in particular, and combat in general. Kahara himself had gotten his first taste of the latter seconded to Anvil while he was still a novice, firing off a few shots to fend off a desultory counterattack. They didn’t do that anymore. It was straight into armor and into the breach.

Or she would be dead. Their armor was tough – nightmarishly tough, frontally – but heavy weapons could punch straight through it all the same. Explosives would crush and pulp what was inside – a weird shiver passed through him at the thought. Even something so seemingly simple as a poor angle might expose the weak joint armor or rear panels to an unlucky shot from man-portable stuff. 

And then there was falling. Falling was every breacher’s nightmare, and the first thing they learned to avoid. It sheared off the momentum right off the attack. It left you helpless and stationary until you managed to climb back up, giving the enemy the time to bring their worst to bear. And Kera had been falling down a lot indeed until only a few weeks ago.

***

The noise changed in tone. The carrier was off the road now, and travelling faster. The engine was straining to put out enough power to the drive motors. Tracks clattered over rocks and roots. They were beginning their approach.

They, and the five other squads spread across the neck of the valley. They were the pressure points, but thousands of men and women would be moving to first build up that pressure and then follow through when something burst. In the hills surrounding them, mortars were positioned. Anvil would be priming shells, smoke and some high explosive to keep the opposition hunkered and blind. The large-bores would be sighted for approach-clearance submunitions. Furnace would be flying unmanned overwatch, and preparing the thousand-tonne support train to follow.

In front of him, Kera was checking the safety of her rifle. Yes, they would be the pressure. Kahara just hoped none of the third would burst under it. His left hand climbed to the hammer on his neck. 

_The Forge tempers us all as we pass through its heat and flame._

It should have been his right hand on the talisman, but he couldn’t let go of his rifle without it falling. The shield he could support against his leg just fine, and it was too wide to fall in the cramped bay in any case. Every curve and corner of the hammer was intimately familiar to him, even through his gauntlet. He didn’t feel the scrapes and chips he knew were there, but the overall shape was glass-clear. He took a glance at the corner of his visor, checking that his transmitter was closed.

Kahara had told his story to anyone who would listen. Or couldn't escape, sometimes. He’d told them of his mishaps in the mountains and on his bike. Of his five siblings, the largest family in three foundries. Of the first time he was drunk, and of the hours-long tirade he had received from his mother the next morning, head splitting from pain.

He hadn’t told anybody of the Forge. Nobody had asked, and Kahara felt they wouldn’t have understood. They would have thought he was praying, or asked him about his god or something. It wasn’t like that.

A murmur on his ears raised him from his thoughts. Kahara might not pray, but Joric most certainly did. Kahara hoped he would say some words for Kera, too. His section leader clearly had a god, and that god seemed to accept prayers. Kahara didn’t really believe it would help, but it certainly couldn’t hurt either. If there was even a sliver of a chance it helped, it was worth the breath. Joric didn’t speak much, but he had never been shy about his faith in the god from the south. Maybe his religion called for it. Maybe it also called for him to leave his squad channel open every time they were making their approach.

***

‘Thirty ticks to ramp.’ Aest’s voice was steady, the words precise. He was always so pure-process on a breach. It was funny, really, considering how well he shot words around with Kahara and Ott, otherwise. Everybody reacted in a different way.

Kahara ran his mind through the first steps. They were always the most critical. A stumble could get the entire squad killed. Clear the ramp and spread out behind the plough. Obscure and walk. Prioritize threats and eliminate, one by one. Always reposition. Always walk.

They all knew the objective. Take the hamlet, deny the anti-air capacity stationed in it, capture intact what they could. They had seen the recon footage from the preparatory high-altitude passes. But, breaches had a nasty habit of surprising you. A rigid plan killed you, Dalen kept saying. Better to work from first principles rather than blueprints. Kahara agreed, and he had a natural dislike of planning in general, too.

A blast slammed Kahara into his seat. His ears rang, even with the helm. A missile had hit the ram, most likely. The smoke must have parted briefly, driven by the earlier than usual morning wind, and given somebody with a launcher a line. Before him, Kera looked like a startled animal. He couldn’t see her face, of course, but her posture spoke of the split-second indecision between freezing and running. 

Kahara grabbed his shield, taking a deep breath through his nose. He held it a moment, and then exhaled through his mouth, lips lightly pursed. He had found it helped to keep his heart rate in check, and his hands steady. Again, it was something that everybody did differently.

Another boom, this one deeper. The carrier veered and then corrected. A heavy mortar had hit somewhere close. A single tube, fired blind. There was no way to actually aim _at_ the carrier with indirects, not with the smoke and the flight time and the path that Aest was weaving. The crew bay shook as the vehicle barged through the last obstacles.

Then the ramp was down and Kahara was moving out.

***

Barely two steps onto the ramp, Kahara felt his foot catch on something. The momentum of his armor kept pushing him forwards, and for a horrified millitick he thought he would fall chest-down right there. Everybody would be trapped behind him. An extra half-step barely got him back to balance. He adjusted his gait, to be ready for Kera stepping on his heels a second time. She didn’t.

Then he was down on the ground and turning right. The few steps from the ramp to the corner of the plough were always awkward. He had to lead with his shield angled, to catch any flanking fire and ricochet it into the carrier. It had taken him weeks of practice to get the sideways shuffle hammered down, and a few embarrassing falls besides. More material for his stories.

He stopped where the plough ended. The wait was always rough. His peripheral view showed the twin barrels of Aest’s belt-fed crusher slotting into the cut in the plough. The barrels shifted a little as Aest found his stance.

Kahara forced in a deep breath and tensed for the signal. His body screamed at him to move. The wait stretched and stretched. He felt exposed. Vulnerable. He knew the logic. He knew Ott would lob a flash cluster over the plough. Knew he and Proto – Kera – would move together. He knew Aest would loose a long, suppressing burst across the entire frontal arc, governor overridden, eight rounds a tick. Knew it would all happen at once. The procedure had been measured and analyzed and optimized multiple times over.

An exhale. It quivered a little. He could only see his forearms and his shield. There were abrasions on the handle, spots of silver against dull grey. He would have to re-coat it soon.

The signal.

Kahara accelerated into motion, smooth, pivoting left as he did. His shield turned against the axis of his advance. Air punched into his diaphragm, again and again. Aest was firing his burst. The smoke flickered, reflecting the dazzler on his shield. A few stronger flashes bounced back at him. The last few pops from Ott’s cluster going off. His eyes scanned the environment, a rapid sequential slicing they all drilled from the beginning. See everything, expect nothing. Most of the breach was hidden by rolling banks of obscurant, but he did see the low vague shapes of the buildings closest to him. A steep roof with wooden shingles cut above the grey fog somewhere further away. Rotten, a part of him noted.

Rifle bullets slammed into his shield. Muzzle flashes lit up the smoke from within. Something hit his pauldron, the sound carried right into his ears by his armor’s substructure, like an anvil dropping on steel. The suit frame sang from delayed resonance. A red gauge lit up in his upper field of vision, indicating the vague sector the shots had come from. It spanned almost the entire width and its edges faded to near invisibility. The squad was still too bunched up for their microphone arrays to triangulate the source in any more detail. A weak bubble of lighter red noted the most likely bearing.

He was getting into the breach now, in his head. The helm whistled and chimed warnings and signifiers in his ear. His gauntlets ticked and tapped with pressure, and the helm kept throwing gauges and needles at him. Kahara let it all flow over him. His earlier tension remained, tight as ever, but he had risen above it. Below his conscious thoughts, he sorted the collated symbols cascading in his vision, evaluated every amplified sound of a sand dripping down a trench and each shift of a shadow within the smoke, and flicked and squeezed his conclusions for the entire squad to see. He worked faster than he would have ever believed before, when he was just a boy with five siblings and a penchant for getting into trouble. If only his teachers saw him now.

Kahara fired back six shots as he walked. He aimed at the horizon level, the reflected reticle on his sight a few ticks to the left from what his visor claimed. He had felt the shots coming from that direction, and he trusted his senses more than the suit sensors. His rifle shoved violently into his armor after every squeeze of his finger, pushing his entire upper body back. Partially obscured by his shield, each shot sent out flames jumping sideways from the muzzle brake. The blast picked up dust from the ground and sent the smoke curling in twin opposing eddies, like a stream winding around a boulder.

The battle flowed around Kahara, and he began to shape it. He saw the buildings appear from the smoke, and took in the magnified aural mass of boots shifting and equipment belts clinking inside it. There. The ambush would hit when he was by that upturned cart there, so he pumped out a third of his magazine through the wall _now_, forcing them to break, letting him face the probable emplacement there inside the smoke shield-on, unless they relocated in which case…It went on and on and on, like a game of Wall where the real play happened beyond the first two cards visible to all, only now there were three tables full of cards and the most ultimate stakes.

He was taking more fire. It hammered into his armor and shield. It rose and intensified, from a few scattered taps to a sharp continuous beat, like hundreds of the biggest crackers Cooling’s Eve going off at once inside his skull and jaw. Kahara walked straight into it. Aest and Joric advanced with him, behind and in pace. His optics highlighted the muzzle flashes, fading red on each pinprick of white. Their rifles boomed in reply. The shots were fewer in number but vastly more powerful than what they bore down on them, an ongoing staggered percussion like a shop full of hammers at full steam. 

With each round, the incoming fire slackened. Kahara used the moment’s lull to briefly bring up his visor map. His section formed a staggered wedge of triangles. He knew they would, and it wasn’t why he had brought the display up. His eyes went to the icons of the first section, instead. To the first one. It was still moving, still holding position with the two others. Good.

The pounding stopped as he reached the shadow of a building. Its absence felt loud, somehow. Breath rasped through his teeth. The turbine howled. A scream in the distance. A sudden eddy of wind rustled around him and cleared off the last remnants of the smoke. Next to his shoulder, a wall of brick rose in a dismal state of disrepair. The mortar had crumbled from between the bricks. The typical mix of inscribed profanities and declarations of presence or love covered the surface. An identical wall rose opposite to it, and together they formed a narrow alley.

He stomped along its length. Ahead, half a dozen spans away, it opened into a wider stretch of space. The main lane bisecting the hamlet, from the maps and high-altitude images.

It was then, as he was just about to round the corner, when he saw it. On the opposite side stood a pile of sandbags, layered in two concentric rings. A barrel protruded from it, wrist-thick, catching a beam of light that had found its way in through the smoke. A nest. It was well positioned, enfilading the lane and leaning against another building for cover.

And it was aimed straight at the first section. At Kera, on point, who was about to walk straight into its arc.

Kahara acted before the realization had fully formed in his mind. His arms pushed forward, fists clenching, a spotting gesture that would be shared and evaluated by every processor on his squad. His trigger finger curled, even if he knew the shot would be too late, that the bullet would bury itself harmlessly into the sand. His mouth opened for a desperate cry of a warning.

‘*K*–.’

Everything slowed down. His throat had only constricted to form the beginnings of the first phoneme when the gun spoke. A flame the length of an arm leapt from the barrel, its fingers curling from and around the perforations at the end. Sparks flew and slowed, spinning in the wavefront of displaced air. The retort reached him a fraction later, a deep rolling thunder that seemed to go on and on. It wasn’t unlike Aest’s crusher. Sharper, perhaps, the gun trading momentum for velocity, but immensely powerful all the same.

His core went cold as the shot punched into Kera. A headshot, almost dead center on her helm. A cloud of shock-fractured ceramics buffed from it. The transferred momentum sent it slamming to the side, maximum rotation. Kahara thought he saw her thermal mount spin through the air. Then she was falling back, her joints loose. Her shield was dropping. Her gun-arm flailed, the barrel drawing an arc through the remnants of the smoke. 

A wash of static replaced all sound and distorted his displays. Through it, Kahara thought he could hear a bell tolling in the distance. Glass crunched beneath his soles.

His fists clenched so tight it hurt. His gauntlets howled in complaint as they squeezed against his shield and rifle handles. Her first breach. He’d already started thinking of her as theirs, and she had clearly had potential, only recently starting to emerge from the awkward beginnings. They wouldn’t be gathering at the tavern today. They would sit forgeside instead, saying whatever words of her that they felt fit. Kahara knew he would choke on his. Later still, he would sneak back to the forge, alone. He would mark his face with its ash and then mend something broken, or just reclaim the metal for later. 

Then, suddenly, her shield snapped back up. _Embers_, that was fast. She crouched, leaning into the next burst. The blows ate into her shield and forced her back a step, but it was a stable retreat now. Controlled, every step where it needed to be, her legs and body forming a solid support for the incoming force to waste itself on. She had it. Kahara released the breath he had been holding. It was shakier than it had been at the plough.

Joric issued a command over the radio, his voice quiet but level. A tick passed, and then pressure was punching into his belly again. The sandbags caved in, and then exploded from within. He turned his eyes before he saw too clearly what was exploding with them. Still, even after all this time, Aest’s 19-mil was terrifying in power.

Kahara stood, waiting for his legs to steady themselves, and watched. Smoke was creeping into the courtyard. Aest and Ott must have lobbed new shells and the obscurant was thickening anew. Soon, it would conceal the six breachers within itself. Further away, the sounds of gunfire were melting away. 

Kahara walked into the grey clouds and took the opportunity to sort through the pool of amber cautions that had accumulated in the corners of his visor. A thermal notice from his shield-arm circuit, a seismic warning that was obviously false, and two dozen other messages he had been too occupied to acknowledge earlier. A moderate-strength chemical trace had been getting stronger as they advanced, composition matching the propellant of an air-defence missile. They were getting close. His audio picked up urgent whispers from the building on the right. He fired a few rounds through the wall, ceiling-level. Inside, footsteps scattered after a yelp. A rifle clattered on the floor.

He turned around the corner. The building lined the central courtyard, a ten-span disk as dilapidated as the rest of the place. The missile truck was standing on it, front wheels spinning and skidding on the cobblestones as it tried to leave. A mossy fountain was leaking water on a fresh pile of rubble, and an ugly dent on the truck’s bumper marked where it had collided. Threat brackets popped up around the vehicle a heartbeat later.

His thumb flicked the magazine selector to the left one, special-purpose. He put a shot through the windshield, the last regular slug from the chamber. The impulse sent the barrel recoiling into the chassis of his rifle. Springs and dampers slid inside, visible through the cooling slits. The stroke seemed a few mils shorter than nominal. Dirt on the rails, most likely. The receiver assembly almost slid to a stop before a thumb-thick case shot out from the oversized ejector port. Definitely dirt.

The windshield cracked from his first shot. Kahara adjusted his reticle down below the dashboard level, and fired a second. Something dark splattered on the cracked glass. An explosive round, and the delayed fuze had gone off low inside the cabin. His third round, a cover-piercing bullet through the engine block, was pure redundancy.

The truck reversed into a corner. Its rear body crumbled, and bricks tumbled down from the wall. The drive wheels kept turning for a moment, digging into the gravel. Then the engine stuttered a few times, and finally stalled with a choked cough, spewing a sooty cloud from the exhaust chimney. It was suddenly quiet.

The red brackets remained. Stupid processor couldn’t see it was over.

Kahara walked and read the flow.

He could see or hear no contacts. The visor had come to the same conclusion at some point, and the bracket for the truck was fading. A flick of a finger brought up the map, again to check the first section. All the icons were still green and moving. He turned his head towards the first one. Leaving a blind angle like that was technically risky and suboptimal, but he needed to know. Besides, the place had gone cold.

Kera barged out through a ruined wall as he watched. An ugly crater in the upper quadrant sullied the upper quadrant of her helm. Forge damn it, that had come close. She seemed to be on the verge of collapsing, her armor swaying and her knees at a weird angle. Hope she hadn’t gotten a concussion, or something bleeding into her brain. Dalen would be checking her readings, and the Furnace nurses would be giving her a thorough evaluation afterward. Kahara would just have to trust that.

A moment passed before Dalen issued a command to regather at the ram. Kahara kept his eyes fixed on Kera a little longer still. The morning breeze had stiffened, and visibility was quickly improving. Satisfied that she seemed steadier, he pivoted and started his way back. His left shoulder was making a scraping sound, and the muscle underneath hurt. He didn’t feel wetness anywhere – and could still _feel_, for that matter – so nothing had come through. Still, he’d been hit by something heavy at some point. Probably the sledgehammer impact by the first building, he thought. It had slipped by his shield and glanced from his shoulder, chips punching the fiber liner into his flesh. He would have to disassemble the entire unit in the afternoon, valve array and all, to see that nothing was bent or cracked. 

***

They were back in the crew bay, secure in their harnesses. Kahara’s ears rang with echoes of chimes, and symbols still ghosted on his eyes. A finger twitched into a barely-suppressed flick. 

Kera was sitting opposite him. She was still, now. Too still, not the slightest whirr or click from her suit. He wondered what was going on in her head. She could be…intense. She always laughed the loudest at his jokes, even those that made Ott roll his eyes and Dalen mutter about an extra watch. Not like the girls at the tavern did, when they were both going through their mutual dance of jests and innuendos that neither would bother to remember the next morning. A heartfelt laugh, face reddening and with tears on her cheeks. The same tears she’d been holding back when she failed Shield A for the second time. She’d dominated it in the end, of course, after pouring her everything into the process.

He wondered if she would laugh too hard today, or not at all. He had leaned towards the former, giggling at everything after his first few breaches. Ott and Aest had been quiet instead. Aest in particular had taken it hard and been near-mute for close to a week. About Joric, he didn’t know. He couldn’t be much more quiet, but Kahara couldn’t imagine him prattling, either.

The carrier rumbled, its tone less urgent. Speed was no longer essential. Koharsk A was theirs. No, that wasn’t correct. Koharsk A had been weeks ago, when Kera was still a tile-cracking Proto and Jessa had joined them instead. This was Koharsk B. They had knocked out its radar and anti-air, cracking their bulb of airspace control. The valley was aligned. Anvil would soon repoint the mortar and fix the rotten roofs with sheet metal. Maybe the broken fountain would be fixed, too.

There were so many breaches, and Kera would be breaching with them. He wondered when they would start blurring together for her. Everybody remembered their first. If they were still alive. His hand strayed to the hammer on his neck, this time without him noticing.

_The Forge both softens and hardens._

The Forge was…everything. It turned raw into useful, and broken into fixed. It had made the tools to raise crops on poor soil, and the weapons to fend off the bandits and raiders of the old.

Its heat marked the passage to the afterworld, some said. Kahara wasn’t sure about that. He knew he might find out on any breach, but he was doing his best to postpone that eventuality all the same. Each ride back to the garrison was marked by the relief of having survived, apart from the few when he was too numb to feel anything. Today, however, everything was tinted by Kera’s close call with the crew-served. He shivered at the memory of her head snapping back in a cloud of shattered armor.

_The Forge burns away the impurities._

Kahara wouldn’t mind if the Forge burned away that particular sight. He would probably dream of it tonight, or perhaps of a cracked windshield turning black. Or it might be Kera bringing up her shield instead. There was something about her. Something unyielding. It had been there from the beginning, he realized, just well hidden by her initial ungainliness. He wasn’t a master-grade people-reader like the counsellor back at the garrison was, but he saw that much. Or feel, more like, like one felt good iron from bad. Something, some weight or texture in her told him she had it in her to be more than just a breacher. The company master had it, and perhaps Dalen too. He most definitely did not, Kahara thought, chuckling inside his helmet. He would remain a shield carrier for as long as he served.

The noise changed once more. A different road, at a different speed. Kahara needed to focus. They were nearing the garrison. Once more, he went through the steps in his mind.

***

For Kahara, the breach always ended in the hangar. The ramp sliding down was like a pressure relief valve going off, or like powering down the lathe after a particularly finicky maintenance session. A part of him even wished that Kera would step on his heel on the ramp again, that he would fall this time. He doubted Kera would find it as funny as everybody else, though. A sliver of unease did pass through him when he left the shadow of the plough. It was the most critical instant on a breach, and his eye noted the new craters and gouges in the edge of the blade. It didn’t last long. The exposed feeling left him as his steps carried him right, to the rear of the carrier and the tool storage, replaced by more urgent matters. He left his rifle there, in the rack, after double-checking that the chamber was empty. The shield could lean against the track guard. He took what he had come for from the storage and stepped around the corner.

‘Helms off,’ came Dalen’s voice over the squad link, taking his mark from Kahara’s appearance. It was a little rough, like it always was after a breach. Kahara hadn’t heard a third of it, but he knew the master had been issuing orders to the squad and communicating with the company command and Furnace for the entire time. Kahara made no move to remove his helm.

Kera was standing next to the carrier, gauntlets working the neck clamps. Her rifle was on the floor, resting on her shield next to her, magazines removed. Her helm came off as Kahara watched, slow and careful. Loose hair fell on her face. She insisted on keeping it neck-length, the maximum allowed. Her pupils were too wide, flickering from Ott to Kahara and around the hangar. Kahara was watching her through his visor. Everybody was.

‘Stand to, novice.’ That was Dalen again, through his suit’s loudspeaker. Kera snapped to attention, a lash of armor-damped motion followed by utter stillness. Her eyes set too, fixed to some particular rivet or a stain on the wall. Kahara saw her left eyelid flutter a little, and the corner of her mouth ticked once. Something, a nerve or a part of her brain, resisted her otherwise perfect control.

They all stood for a moment. Kera’s breathing was visible from the slight movements of her neck and nostrils. It was still breach-fast. Kahara decided to slip his own breathing technique into some discussion later. It helped some, and it might help her too. Or she might not need any help at all. The rest of the squad stood clay-sculpture still, the way only suited breachers could. They were silent, their turbines off. The carrier ticked as it bled off the heat of the journey. Something clattered. A chip of ceramics falling from someone’s suit, bouncing around the floor and then coming to rest somewhere in their midst.

An electric whine broke the silence. Dalen was turning his helmet, Kahara knew. Nodding at him.

A few steps closed the distance that had separated him from Kera. His footfalls sounded thunderous in the quiet, the metallic slam reverberating from the sheet metal walls. He stopped a pace from her. She remained still, eyes looking straight through him. Kahara was suddenly happy about his obscuring visor. He brought up his gauntlet. It rose with a structure-carried whine, almost like it was coming from inside _him_. Then the shoulder hydraulics locked, and it was all quiet again.

The plaque rested on his open palm, dark red-brown on brushed grey. The steel base was from a carrier backplate, cut with a torch and then ground so that the edges didn’t cut the hand. On it, they had glued a red practice tile. A treeroot crack pattern cut it into three. It was one Kera’s, from one of her first nights in the squad. They had certainly had an abundance of broken tiles to choose from, Kahara thought with a smile. He had distracted Kera when she was replacing the cracked plates, while Joric had palmed the tile with practiced ease. Joric out of all of them. Kahara couldn’t figure him out. 

‘At ease,’ came Dalen’s voice again, distorted by the speaker. ‘Take it.’

Kera glanced at him, as if for confirmation. Kahara couldn’t tell if she received any. For a heartbeat, her eyes flipped to Kahara, boring through the visor. Then she reached out and took the plaque. A little scraping sound carried through his suit as it brushed against his gauntlet, her fingers wrapping around it.

‘To remember where you came from.’ It was Ott’s voice issuing from a speaker now, all according to tradition. Kera’s section would do the speaking. Kahara had said a few words when Aest joined, and before that it had been Joric’s terse address while Kahara stood unhelmed and tried to figure out what was happening. Kera was holding the plaque across her lap and looking at Ott’s armored form.

‘And to remember that armor cracks,’ Dalen finished, his voice softer, even distorted by the speaker. ‘To remain humble. Careful.’ Kahara tried to not think about a cloud of armor shards flying.

Then Dalen was all command again, issuing a command to unhelm, for real this time, given over the link. Kahara got his off first, having stowed his gun and shield earlier. The air was cool on his face, and he felt he saw and heard best without the fifteen kilos of armor and equipment around his head, despite all the audio pickups and visual sensors built into it. It was stupid, he knew. In front of him, Kera was turning the plaque on her gauntlets, slow and careful. As if she was inspecting the workmanship, or finally finding out where that particular tile had gone.

‘We etched our names to the back. Old maker’s marks.’ Kera looked up from the tile at his words, up from the tile and then down at him. She was smiling now. ‘Not all squads do that, but it’s been a tradition of the third since…don’t know. Master?’

‘No idea. They did it in my time, too.’

‘Since the Foundry States, then.’ Kahara felt Ott rolling his eyes. Maybe he’d actually get that extra watch tonight. Worth it. Kera flipped the tile around, eyes widening a fraction as she found the lettering. Her lower lip quivered a little. For a moment, the silence stretched again.

‘Maintenance now,’ Dalen went. ‘Move out, breachers.’ Five suits of armor moved in unison, locking helms to hips and picking up weapons. Clamps locked shut, hundreds of kilos of material scraped and brushed against itself, and electromagnetic torque and hydraulic pressure pushed damped bootsoles against concrete.

In the midst of it, Kera remained standing at rest. Her eyes switched between the plaque and Dalen, expectant. Kahara watched her from the corner of his eye, trying and failing not to smile. He wondered how long it would take. Then the realization hit, and her face lit. Fast, again. He had stood like an idiot for at least half a minute when it was him holding the commemorative plaque. Hurriedly, she stowed it in her utility pouch, and bent over to pick her rifle and shield. It all happened in one, fluid movement. Not a trace of conscious balancing showed.

The clumsy novice was gone. She was Kera.
