Branik dropped down from the trackbed, his hand on the handle for support. Immediately, the vehicle roared and lurched forward, drivewheels squealing, nearly pulling him off balance. Where was it? He looked around, and only remembered to drop to one knee when the vehicle passed. A crater, he was searching for a crater, in a cratered hillside with the visibility still down from all the smoke and particulates.

A black and gray cloud mushroomed into the air behind the earthworks wall, a hundred spans uphill. The boom-crack of it reached him a fraction later. Branik flinched. This was far worse than he remembered. As if on cue, a sound like a box of ball bearings poured on plywood, building from the first pop of a gunshot into an overlapping roll in the span of a heartbeat. It died as quickly but the fact remained. He was too exposed, too visible. If they still had active units, if somebody fell back his way–there! 

He ran towards the crater, hunched over and expecting a bullet to gut him at any moment. Two trackbeds rumbled past, uncomfortably close. The first an anti-flier variant, the turret on its back with thick twin barrels swept to the side, almost banging his head as it swept past, or so it felt. A bunker-carrier followed. Near the riverbank, a third had uncoiled the spool of barb-wire from its container and was now blade-down, working the ground into an emplacement.

He stumbled into the crater and slumped onto his back. Breath sawed in and out of his lungs, and he could taste blood at the back of his throat. Not that young anymore, evidently.

The breacher lay on his side, gloves fumbling at the neck armor. Furrows in the earth ran from him to a collapsed section of the crater lip, marking where he had been dragged in. His left leg was ramrod-straight and had been extended all the way to the back.

Branik crawled closer.

***

The <battalion> master had asked to see him the evening before yesterday. He came in person, just as Branik was penciling his last notes in the margins of the blueprints. The master bent over to examine his work, commenting here and raising an eyebrow there. Branik could tell right away that he was stalling, meaning something was wrong.

Personnel reallocations should have been the overseer’s duty, really. Branik hadn’t been on the line for two decades, but they needed one more engineer. The maze of dells and dales was close to folding already, so they needed to keep up the pressure. Branik would ride in the consolidation train to verify the integrity of the bridge and oversee any repairs needed. Heavy armor would then roll over the bridge and complete the flanking move, but it needed to do so fast.

A day and a half later, and a platoon master made her way to Branik in the lurching crew bay. She moved hand over hand on the guardrails, stepping over and around the knees of boys and girls half Branik’s age as the trackbed shook them around. Branik had to ask her to repeat herself twice in the clamor.

‘–down. Need an engineer.’

‘But the bridge?’

‘This takes priority, master,’ she said, tapping at the radio on her chest. ‘We’ll drop you in two. One hundred from objective, bearing zero-one-five. Got that?’

Branik nodded hurriedly and tried to remember the maps. He had memorized the topography of the riverbanks and the estimated depth to bedrock around the bridge, west of the gatefort guarding it. Now, he was dropping to the south instead, blind.

***

The breacher pulled off his helm, and long blond hair spilled out. Branik nearly stopped in his tracks. Anvil strike units, used as speartips and for counter-attacks, were nine-tenths young men. Hauling around all the woven armor and ceramics, let alone the gun and all the equipment, required an extreme level of fitness, and biology was merciless on that tail end of the distribution. But then, none of that really applied to breachers. They were–

‘Something wrong?’ the woman put down her helm next to her and craned her neck to see Branik better despite the awkward angle, propped away from him.

‘Master?’ There was a clear edge of pain to it, yet the woman’s voice easily carried over the rumble of the hundred tons of equipment already moving in their vicinity.

A woman’s voice, but only barely so. The alignment front wasn’t an old person’s world, but she was from the opposite end of the spectrum. Barely old enough to join, or so she sounded. But then again, she was wearing the armor and thus was mature enough to wear it, and had all the other required traits besides. 

Branik crawled closer, flat against the crater. Jagged rocks bit into his knees and elbows. The smell of spent explosives and powdered rock was overpowering and made his eyes water. 

‘You are the engineering support requested?’ the breacher prompted. ‘Because I would appreciate it if you could get started rather than cower like that. That lip covers us from everything up to light crew-served, and they can’t get an angle on us in the first place. I would prefer to get on with my day. And I am in some amount of pain.’

Branik stopped and got up on his elbows, eyeing the crater. The breacher was right. They were deep in it, and the displaced earth and rocks around the lip formed an extra layer of protection. Besides, sounds of combat were dying, and being replaced by trenchdiggers and ditch-makers at work. Branik felt suddenly foolish.

He saw the breacher better, too. Half a dozen impacts from small arms were scattered over the armor. Its left hip had been pulverized by something much heavier. The panels had cracked into thumb-sized chips, and it looked like the backplate had been caved in. Branik’s gut clenched at the sight. If the blast had got through, then the half a decade he had spent working as an armor overseer in his twenties would be an entirely wrong kind of expertise.

Yet it was the face that drew his attention. She was beautiful. Branik noticed it almost despite himself. He rarely noticed such things. But it was not what he had noted first. Everything about her screamed Old Foundry, from the poise of her jaw to the expression that remained measured even when lying wounded and possibly dying at the bottom of a mortar crater. The inked mark on her cheek was an unnecessary finishing touch.

Branik had had the misfortune to speak to people of her ilk on numerous instances, none of which had been pleasurable. Oh, they were polite, painfully so, and exactly in the same way one was polite to the tram driver in the shift-change rush. Or to a particularly well-functioning lathe. And it didn’t seem like this time was going to be any different.

‘Aye, I’m the support, I guess. Name’s Branik.’

‘Master Branik.’ The breacher craned her neck again to nod. Her eyes locked with Branik’s for exactly as long as was required, and not a millitick longer. ‘I am Jessa.’

She slumped back and grimaced before continuing.

‘Apologies if I sounded rude earlier, Master, but as stated, I am in some pain.’

‘The hip? Looks rather beaten to me. Maybe a medic would–’

‘That is quite unnecessary. Something shorted the medical subsystem, but I am quite certain I am not bleeding, at least not severely, and I have still got full function in my leg. And they would need to get the armor off first, which brings us exactly to why you are here in the first place, master.’ 

Branik looked at the mangled hip again. Yes, definitely deformed backplates. The latches securing the armor sections would be rammed tight.

‘Stuck?’ he asked, just for confirmation.

‘In more than one way. I can’t remove the armor, and the actuator has seized. The leg worked fine until we were regrouping and then ran into full extension and never came back.’

The breacher turned her head away a fraction before she continued, her voice more subdued now.

‘Something snapped, and it bit into me. My, um, uppermost posterior thigh.’

‘It bit your upper–you mean your butt?’

‘Yes, master. It bit me in the butt.’

Branik turned his head and stifled a laugh. The breacher wasn’t a pleasure to work with, but otherwise the day had the makings of an amazing story. Perhaps even a case study, too.

‘And did it release its bite?’

‘It did not. It cannot be deep, but it hurts like a–it hurts, as I did mention.’

‘I see.’

‘Perhaps you could get to it now, master?’

Slag, but the lady was eating at his nerves. It wasn’t even what she said, but…he didn’t know. He focused on calming down, formulating an appropriate answer in his head when a pebble skittered down the crater wall. Then another, and a third, all kicking off their own minuscule rockslides. The exposed ground vibrated under Branik. A heavy vehicle, close and getting closer.

The breacher lady didn’t look worried in the least, so Branik dared a look over the lip. Quick and low, next to a head-sized lump of clay, not silhouetting himself against the background. He remembered that much.

It was a breach carrier. _The_ breach carrier, most likely, the one that had carried this squad in. It stopped some distance away and executed a neutral turn, tracks churning up what remained of the grass. Something squealed, and as it turned its other flank towards the crater, Branik could see the telltale signs of mine damage. At least two of the road wheels were bent out of alignment, and probably causing the unhealthy noise. The engine sounded like it had taken a hit, too.

‘Your squad’s here. Think they have a potforge? I might need one soon.’

The breacher picked up her helm again and lifted it a finger’s width away from her face. It would break her nose if her glove slipped now.

‘I’ll have Aest bring it from the ram. Or whoever might be the closest.’

***

Branik yanked and the armor section snapped loose, sending the final loose shards of ceramic rattling to the ground. The breacher’s entire armor convulsed a little and she cried out. She bit down on it halfway through the sound, but it was still enough to startle Branik and make him nearly drop the pry-bar.

‘You alright? Talk to me.’

‘Apologies. Just a stab of pain that I was not expecting.’

The pry bar on the ground now, Branik peered inside the armor with a flashlight. The opening was small, but it was all he could do with the tools available and the rest of the plates wedged tight. It was well possible he wouldn’t even be able to reach the actuator, let alone do anything to fix it.

‘The mount’s shorn loose. Probably just fatigue after the damage from…what was it, actually?’

A bead of sweat had appeared on her forehead.

‘A repeater cannon. It managed to get a burst off before Ott spigoted them. A shell glanced off my shield.’

She had been lucky that the fuze had had enough delay to go off before fully connecting with her hip. Or not lucky enough for it to pass her completely. It depended on how you viewed it.

‘I’m going to have to reach in to feel around a little. Just let me know if you feel anything weird.’

He did, after thoroughly sanitizing his hands. Nearby, another breacher was coaxing the potforge to life. Branik had to squeeze his entire forearm inside the armor to reach far enough. A sharp edge dug painfully into his skin as he felt his way forward. It was slow going.  He built a schematic with his fingertips only and compared it to what he remembered, updating accordingly. Once, a rock came loose and rolled down the crater wall, startling Branik. He’d been so alert for a needle of leaking high-pressure fluid to slice off his fingers that he jumped at the sudden sound and banged his knee. Luckily, the breacher did not seem to notice, or was polite enough not to mention it.

He found the mount right where he expected, and not where it was supposed to be, wet and sticky with clotted blood and bleed-stoppers. Then the valve array, covered in something powdery and abrasive. So the back-plate had indeed been pierced.

‘Found it.’ Branik strained with all the strength he could muster, fingertips squeezed painfully tight. ‘Can’t free it, though.’

He heard and felt Jessa shift, no doubt in a gesture to go on.

‘A shard’s blocking the pressure-boost spool valve open. Not by much, but the pump’s in series with it–’

‘–and the motor could barely fight one third of the pressure. I know how my armor works.’ For the first time, Branik heard a trace of a smile in her voice. ‘Though probably not as well as you do. Please continue, master.’

‘You probably did something to activate the boost, just enough to wedge the shard against the spring and stop it from returning, and the force then tore off the mount.’

‘I tried to do a little jump. I know, I know, but it was good to be back. Good to be with the boys one more time.’

Branik took a moment to massage his cramping forearm. There was no way he was getting the shard out with his fingers only. He needed a tool. Fortunately, the pot was hot by now. Soon, he was on his knees by it, a steel bar already glowing yellow-hot between the embers and the familiar heat on his face. He had missed this.

‘Excuse me if I’m being too direct, master, but you seem a little too experienced to be moving in right after us,’ the breacher said just as Branik began hammering the first idea of a shape into the rod. He finished his striking before he answered. No sense to waste the heat, and he didn’t want to have to shout over the clamour.

‘Thought the same until two days ago. A lack of personnel, nothing more than that. I had almost forgotten what it feels like.’

The curved shape of the bar looked about right. Like the letter J, with enough of a bend to reach the chip but not so much that he couldn’t wiggle it in through the removed armor section. Next, the fork to the end, a final fitting, and then hardening the working end. He plunged it to the coals again, turning it so it would heat evenly.

It had to work. If it didn’t, he was out of options.

‘So a novice got you, too,’ she continued. Branik stopped working the rod. There was a dreamy quality to the girl’s voice now. Just a sliver, barely there, but noticeable. Or had he just missed it earlier?

‘Everything all right? Ma’am?’

‘Please call me Jessa. All is fine. Just lost in my thoughts for a tick. Where was I?’

‘Something about a novice?’

‘Ah, that. I am supposed to be in command apprenticeship right now, would you believe? Got recalled to my old squad for the breach. Some new girl could not learn the suit in time.’ 

Now that was surprising. Branik felt his eyebrows rise. Even with <accelerated schedules> of recent years, anybody chosen for breacher command would have made a master two times over in Anvil. Even the Furnace, though you would never hear them admit it.

‘So you were not supposed to be here in the first place.’

‘Correct. Like I’m not supposed to have a steel beam up my arse.’

She was definitely acting differently now. Maybe it was nothing more than a coming down from stress hormones, the real her. Or a checkpoint along that way, and there was yet more of her to come. He had seen a range of different reactions to combat back in his days, from total shutdown to reckless misbehaviour. Still, better work promptly now, just in case there was something wrong.

The tool fit well enough on the first try. Getting it in place was tight, but nothing that a little force wouldn’t solve. Just the final heat, cherry red, then a quick quench in a water bottle. The final hiss was always the best part, a signature to a work well done, even if the surface finish would have gotten a first apprentice chewed out and sent back to do it again.

It was time.

‘Going to dislodge it now,’ Branik said as he guided the fork in place around the chip, working blind with only the scrapes and taps of contact transferred through the rod to guide him. 

‘The controller should notice the pressure drop and realign the limb. It might hurt, but try to stay still.’ Once the tension was gone from the frame, he could get the rear section open and fix the mount well enough for Jessa to walk. Or limp, at least.

Jessa swallowed and gave her assent.

Branik pushed, and nothing happened. The shard was lodged in tight. Should he try tapping the free end of the tool with his hammer? But then the impact might simply shatter the ceramic and leave the tip inaccessible. Maybe he should ask one of the breachers to help.

The tool snapped loose and into the insides of the suit. Slag it, he had broken the shard. Or maybe the tool had simply lost its grip.

Then, a whine and a faint whirr. The motor kicking in. Jessa grunted and twitched, then drew a shaky breath.

‘Congratulations, master. And well done.’ 

She sounded tired. Tired but happy. Branik eased the tool out, careful not to manhandle her leg any more. She had definitely been through enough. He’d give her a moment to recover and then remove the rear section. Maybe a dressing on her wound, from the kit on his thigh pocket, a dab of quick-cure resin on the mount. Then he could go on to the actual matter of the bridge, which would ideally take no more than an hour, after which he could catch a ride back on any of the trucks and be home by–

***

‘Ummm, master? I don’t feel too good.’

The girl had gone pale, and there was a faint blue tinge on her lips that definitely hadn’t been there before. That couldn’t be good. Branik turned his eyes on the hip module again and froze. There was something dripping through the cracks in her crushed armor. Dark drops oozing out from half a dozen spots at once. Slowly, each drop taking its time to form, but terrifyingly fast when summed together.

Blood.

Had he cut her? Maybe when the tool slipped. It didn’t matter now, though. He rushed back, the tool clattering against the loose rocks as he dropped it. His voice called for help.

Then he was on his knees again, wiggling his arm inside her armor. The edge digging into his forearm felt like nothing this time. He felt his way forward, searching with his fingers, this time for cuts in the undersuit, where the tool had been. Nothing. Had to be the cut from the mount then. If he squeezed his hand under the leg strut, between it and her thigh, he could perhaps reach it.

He did. His fingers were all warm and wet from blood by then, as was ‌most of his palm. More was pouring out of the wound. He felt it flowing around his fingers. It was steady, not pumping in beat with the heart. That was a good thing, right?

He pressed against the wound, his fingers and arms stretched to the maximum. The muscles shook already. It wasn’t much, but it was the best he could do.

Something heavy landed next to him. A breacher. Bent over as he was over Jessa’s leg, elbow-deep inside the suit, Branik could only see up to its neck ring. A small hammer hung from it. Then it knelt down, a face with friendly eyes unhelmed.

‘What do you need, master?’

‘I think she’s bleeding,’ Branik blurted. ‘We need a medic. A proper one.’

He took a breath to steady himself and continued.

‘Can you get the rear section off? Hip unit. See better, that way.’ He felt as if he had just been running to the crater again.

‘What’s wrong? Why won’t you talk to me?’ It was Jessa, her voice urgent now. To his shame, Branik suddenly realized he had no idea how long she had been talking.

The other breacher put his fingertips on her cheek. Again, an impossibly gentle gesture with the massive glove.

‘You’re bleeding a little, but a medic is two minutes out. We’ll take care of you until then, together with the master here.’ He lingered a moment longer, then set off to work on removing the armor section.

By then, Branik had gathered enough of his wits to continue.

‘I think the mount nicked a vein. I’m pressing the wound now. We’ll take off the rear hip armor now, to work better.’

Jessa didn’t answer. Branik tried to press harder, willing the other breacher to work faster. For the blood to clot better. Something.

Then two things happened at once. A clasp clicked, and the armor section came loose. And a torrent of blood gushed out. It sounded like a flash flood building up when it hit the rocks underneath.

A part of Branik had been getting ready for the worst, whatever it might be, and kicked into action. He wrenched ‌his arm from the armor. Some of his skin was left behind, another part of him noted. Maybe even some of the underlying tissue. But it mattered not, for he was already pressing against the wound again, able to exert significantly more pressure now that he wasn’t limited by the difficult position any more. His other hand had already pulled the aid kit from his pocket and brought its edge to his mouth, working together with his teeth to get the transparent cover open. Probably highly suboptimal from the pathogen transmission point of view, but he wasn’t exactly flooded with options here. The cover came loose, his fingers sorted through the contents one-handed, found the dressing. Passed it to his other hand. Press.

Time rushed back. The breath sawing into his lungs. The shake of his fingers.

The sound.

‘I-I’m cold.’ Jessa sounded very young.

‘I think you were bleeding inside your armor from the start. A hefty amount, pooling in there, but nothing dangerous. You just feel its absence now. Evaporative cooling on your undersuit. It’s what it was made for, remember?’

‘Yeah.’

Branik wanted to believe it. That it was a modest loss of blood, enough to look bad but not enough to cause severe shock. He wished he remembered the numbers. How much blood could you lose before it became dangerous?  How would that look when poured into a mortar crater?

At least the bleeding had been reduced to a drip now.

It was quiet.

‘Jessa?’

The answer took too long to come.

‘Yes?’

Good. He should keep her talking, or maybe it would be better to let her rest. What did breachers talk about? Or girls barely out of apprenticeship. The latter was the larger set.

‘Do you like ice cream?’ He was not good at talking.

‘I…What? Yes? Yes, I do.’

Well, he was committed now.

‘What’s your favorite?’

‘Am I dying?’ She made it sound like a joke, but he heard a quiver in her voice. Again, it was barely there, at the edge of hearing.

The other breacher joined in, the one with friendly eyes and the recommended volume of blood.

‘I like honey and nuts. My mom used to buy it. The only thing keeping the herd in check, she’d say. Come on now, Jessa.’

‘Fine, then. I like sour orange. You, master?’

‘I can’t eat milk,’ Branik admitted after a moment’s hesitation.

Branik more felt than heard Jessa sigh.

‘This is the stupidest conversation I have ever had.’

‘She’s right,’ the other breacher said. ‘And the standards are strict in that respect. Or loose. Depends on how you see it.’

Then there was nothing more to speak about. Just listening to Jessa’s breathing, shaky and clearly audible in the relative quiet. But stable. Her blood dripping slowly between Branik’s fingers. Her breath. As long as it kept coming. It would be so slagging unfair to lose her like this.

The sound of running footsteps announced the arrival of the medic a few ticks before the trooper stumbled into the crater and skidded to a halt next to the trio. Another young lady, slightly older than Jessa. She unslung her carbine without a word and knelt beside Branik.

‘Move aside. No no, keep the pressure on. Yes, like that.’ She inspected the wound, brisk but thorough, peeking below the edges of the dressing.

‘You with me, breacher? What’s your name? Got feeling in your toes, Jessa? Uh-huh. Anything else hurting? Normally I’d inspect you myself, but the armor’s in the way.’

Branik could only admire her doing her trade. Her face was expressionless, apart from her mouth which was locked halfway between a sneer and a grimace. She must have had her work cut out for her all morning. Mostly the defenders, and mostly non-critical. Breachers tended to kill their intended targets thoroughly, but there was always someone else nearby to catch a shrapnel or the edge of a shockwave. But it was clear that she cared.

‘This will hurt a little, but the antalgic should kick in in a tick.’ She pushed an injector into the wound. Expanding sealant sponges, Branik guessed. Then a skin seal, all in less than ten ticks.

‘Done. You’ll live.’ She spoke to Jessa and the other breacher in turn, all the while packing her equipment and sanitizing her hands. ‘Jabbed a little stim, too, to help with the shock. Out of fluids. But you need to get her transferred to the field hospital as soon as possible. At the crossing, fifteen kilos that way. Tendons are fine, but avoid harsh motions, or you’ll tear open the wound. Pulse climbs or pressure drops, and you call for aid. Got it all?’

***

Without a further word, she was off again. That had been intense. Not a single word or motion wasted. Did Branik’s own work ever look like that to outsiders? Probably not. Staring at a terminal or annotating a print didn’t really convey the same urgency, even if it was just as important in its own diluted but wide-reaching way.

The male breacher muttered something inaudible and then inclined his head to listen.

‘Dalen’s got you a transport. Ten out. A trackbed. They can hoist you up nice and easy.’ He said after a while.

‘I will walk out of this breach.’ Jessa’s tone wagered no disagreement, yet something in it was…different. The Old Foundry was there, but subdued. In the foundations only, supportive but not defining.

‘I can prop the mount,’ Branik offered. ‘Should do it in any case. You still carry the quick-cure?’

Fixing the mount took all the time they had before the trackbed’s arrival, most of which was spent on watching the resin cure. Like the shard-removal tool, it would not win any praise for its cleanliness or finish, but it would hold until the field hospital. They pulled Jessa back on her boots–the other breacher did, obviously–and she took a few tentative steps. Awkward and careful, though it was hard to say if it was because of her injury, the damage to her armor, or Branik’s fix.

Getting her onto the bed turned out to be a minor challenge on its own, since the suit didn’t have enough range of motion to get her boot on the lowest rung.The vehicle overseer also suggested using the crane. Jessa silenced him with a look, but then added that she would like to maintain a small illusion of dignity after the day’s events, if you would be so kind as to indulge her please. In the end, the solution turned out to be as old as two-limbed motion, with the other breacher kneeling and Jessa using his thigh as a step.

Now she was safely on the bed, and being made safer by the crew fussing around her with ratchet straps. Jessa’s opinion was clear from her expression, but the vehicle overseer was adamantly not having that much unsecured mass between the equipment and the few walking wounded. Branik didn’t blame him. Jessa was perky and attentive, jittery even, but the size of her pupils told it would only last for as long as the drugs in her bloodstream did.

Then she calmed, her face grave.

‘I never thanked you, Branik.’

Branik shrugged and kicked a loose stone. Carbonate, with a light brown iron streak going through it. It bounced against the vehicle’s tracks and skittered into the furrow left by it.

‘I should have noticed the bleeding earlier.’

‘Don’t be so modest. You saved my life.’

‘It’s a possibility. Though I suspect any of your squad could have done as good of a job, if not better.’

‘Not true. It is obvious you know every strut and mount in the armor. You did—’ She was not looking at Branik any more, and her face had turned deadpan. Branik followed her eyes. The other breacher was obviously working very hard to maintain a neutral face. The corner of his mouth was twitching.

Jessa gave a resigned sigh.

‘Gonna save you the trouble, Kahara.’ She turned to Branik again. 

‘Branik, you saved my ass. Rather more literally than I would prefer, too.’ A trace of a smile.

‘I am so happy to be out of here,’ she continued after another sigh.

Now, the other breacher spoke.

‘You don’t mean it. Bite.’

‘Bite?’ Branik asked.

Jessa sighed deeply before answering.

‘The armor bit me. I used the words myself.’

Now the other breacher laughed. Before Branik could say anything more, the vehicle overseer jumped off the bed.

‘All set. Vehicle moving!’ he called out, already running towards the cabin.

Branik sidestepped, away from the vehicle’s path should it reverse, and to make himself visible in the rear-view mirrors. On the other side, the breacher did the same. The engine revved, and the vehicle ground into motion.

On its bed, Jessa met Branik’s eyes and nodded, a solemn smile on her face. Branik returned it.
